The prototype's Audit page is a feed across the whole catalog (D45). Today `GET .../audit` (`AuditReadRoutes`):
- returns one subject's own records, from one partition;
- reaches at most 20,000 offsets back and returns at most 200 per call;
- needs `manage-grants`.

Searching across objects or people was the SIEM's job (build-plan.md, UI v1). Since item 27, the queryable audit
tables (D23) can do it too.

What done looks like:
- A feed per catalog:
  - newest first;
  - filtered by kind, principal, path prefix (a namespace with its tables and views) and time;
  - paged with a cursor;
  - read from the audit tables, so about a minute behind, and the feed says so.
- Each record with its request id, its partition and offset, its neighbours in the hash chain, and whether that link
  was verified.
- The archive check's last result and time.
- The last day's counts by kind and the busiest objects, matching the health page's figures for the same day.
- Who may read it is settled as for the audit tables (D23, item 85). Route tests for each refusal.

What was done (2026-11-22, worktree-agent-ab2dd64ae8c96cf7a):
- Four routes in `AuditReadRoutes`, described in catalog-audit-tables' README:
  - `GET .../catalogs/{catalog}/audit/feed`, with filters by type, principal, path prefix and time and a page token;
  - `.../audit/summary`, the last day's counts;
  - `GET /management/v1/audit/archive`, the archive check's status;
  - `GET /management/v1/audit/chains/{partition}/records/{offset}?event-id=`, a record's place in its hash chain.
- Each needs `select` on the audit catalog (D23, amended 2026-11-22: "Audit readers only"). A refusal is a 403,
  audited as a `Denial` on the audit catalog.
- The feed reads the table `events` through `EventsReader`, new in catalog-audit-tables, linked into the server
  ("Yes, link them"). Each response's `table.committed-at` says how far behind it is.
- The archive check (`ArchiveCheck` in catalog-audit, run by `AuditArchiveCheck`) runs a minute after start and
  hourly after that. Each run verifies only what was archived since the last head it verified, up to the archive
  consumer's anchor. It keeps what it verified in the committed offsets of a consumer group of its own, so every
  server shares it.
- Each feed row says whether its chain is verified past it. The chain route reads the one segment holding the record
  and gives its neighbours' hashes and whether its link holds.
- The counts come from the table. They differ from the server's own counters: the writer leaves its own records out,
  and a record sent to the fallback log reaches neither the topic nor the table. The README says so.
- Choices settled:
  - the archive status is for audit readers too, not platform operators;
  - the feed answers 404 for a catalog that no longer exists (SQL on the tables reaches those records);
  - the counts are kept for a minute and shared.

    claimed;
  - the S3 client duplicates `GovernanceWiring.archive`'s, since item 131 held that file;
  - the console's use of the routes (item 124).
