Item 21 lets a storage profile name a CA bundle (`nuna.storage.profiles.<name>.ca-bundle`), which the profile's
`MetadataFiles` and `CredentialVendor` clients trust beside the JVM's CAs. The audit archive builds a third S3
client from a storage profile (`nuna.audit.archive.profile`) in `GovernanceWiring.archive`, and that one still trusts
only the JVM's CAs: an archive bucket on an on-prem store with a private CA fails its startup `checkBucket` with a
TLS handshake error. Item 21 left it alone because item 19 held `governance/` that night.

Done when `GovernanceWiring.archive` reads the profile's bundle (`CaBundle.read`, refusing an unreadable one with
the setting's name, as `CatalogStorage.caBundle` does) and applies it to its `S3ClientBuilder`
(`CaBundle.applyTo`); a Java-only test against `PrivateCa.serve` (catalog-tests-s3) shows the archive's client
refused without the bundle and accepted with it; and a bite is recorded.

Done: `GovernanceWiring.archive` reads the archive profile's `ca-bundle` with `CaBundle.read` and applies it to the
archive's `S3ClientBuilder` (`CaBundle.applyTo`), so its startup `checkBucket` and its segment writes trust the
bundle beside the JVM's CAs. A bundle it cannot read, or that holds no certificate or a broken one, stops startup
with `nuna.storage.profiles.<profile>.ca-bundle: ` and the file, as `CatalogStorage` does. Without the setting the
client is built as before. `docs/install-at-work.md` section 5 says so.
- Test (Java only, no Spring, no container): `ArchiveCaBundleTest`, 3 tests, against `PrivateCa.serve` answering
  GetObjectLockConfiguration: with the bundle the bucket check reaches the server and the archive starts; without
  it, an `SSLHandshakeException` and no request reaches the server; a missing, empty or broken bundle stops it
  naming the setting and the file. All pass in the fast build.
- Bites, recorded and checked with `git apply --check`, not run: `bites/M7/archive-ca-bundle-ignored` and
  `bites/M7/archive-ca-bundle-unreadable-ignored`.
- Left: nothing in the code. No test runs the archive against a real store behind a private CA (Ceph or MinIO
  with TLS), as for item 21; the in-process server stands in for it.
