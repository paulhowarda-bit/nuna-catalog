Low, from the security review of item 156 (Nuna Security Expert session, 2026-11-23). Not a go-to-work condition.

What is wrong: a purge claim and a removal from refs (item 156) read the holds and freezes on the object, its
namespaces and the catalog inside their transaction, but lock only the object's row. A legal hold placed on a
namespace above the object, or on the catalog, while one of them runs can commit after its read. The result is the
same as the operation finishing first and the hold arriving a moment later (placing a hold reads nothing either
writes, and the hold then stands, so `Rules.retentionHold` keeps main's snapshots and later purge claims are
refused), so nothing is lost; only the audit trail can show the hold's record a little earlier than the operation's.

Done when the purge claim and the removal lock the held levels (or the catalog row) before reading holds, on all four
stores; a store suite case holds a namespace hold's write open while each runs and sees them serialized; bites
recorded.

## Done, 2026-11-25 (branch worktree-agent-ac5aa9e6de1840a97)

A purge claim and a removal from refs now lock every held level before they read holds and freezes, on all four
stores, in the order rule 8 of `docs/storage-design.md` section 9 (f) now gives (the catalog's row first).

- PostgreSQL and CockroachDB:
  - `SqlObjects.claimPurge` (catalog-store-sql/.../SqlObjects.java): reads the object's catalog unlocked, locks the
    catalog's row `FOR SHARE` (line 240), the object `FOR UPDATE` as before, then, after the claimant and due checks,
    the namespaces above it `FOR SHARE` in id order (`Guards.lockLevels`, Guards.java:68; called at line 253), then
    reads holds, freezes and refs.
  - `SqlRefs.remove` (SqlRefs.java): the catalog's row `FOR SHARE` (as before, line 221), each named ref `FOR UPDATE`
    in id order, the object `FOR UPDATE`, then `Guards.lockLevels` (line 235), then holds and freezes.
  - A share lock does not wait for a share lock, and a placement took one, so a hold or a DR freeze now locks its
    target `FOR UPDATE` (`Guards.missingForUpdate`, Guards.java:37; `SqlGovernance.placeHold` line 154,
    `putAttribute` for `DR_FREEZE` line 91). Other attributes, grants and creates keep their share locks.
- Oracle: `OracleObjects.claimPurge` locks the catalog's row (line 248), the object, then the namespaces
  (`Guards.lockLevels`, Oracle Guards.java:62; called at line 262), all for update; `OracleRefs.remove` adds the
  namespaces (line 247) after its catalog, refs and object locks. A placement there already locked its target for
  update.
- MongoDB: `MongoObjects.claimPurge` marks the catalog and the namespaces above the object (MongoObjects.java:341-342)
  as well as writing the object; `MongoRefs.remove` marks the namespaces (MongoRefs.java:507) beside its catalog write.
  A hold or freeze placed on any of those levels marks it, so the two conflict and the one MongoDB runs again reads the
  other's write. `Guards.seeNamespaces` finds and sees them before the stamp is fixed.

Choices made (Paul may overrule):
- On PostgreSQL and CockroachDB the rare write takes the strong lock: a hold or freeze placement locks its target for
  update, and the claim and the removal, which the maintenance worker runs all night, take share locks on the catalog
  and namespaces. The other way round (claims locking the catalog's row for update) would make every claim of a
  catalog wait for every other and for namespace and object creates. A placement holds one lock and waits for nothing
  after it, so it cannot close a deadlock cycle.
- The catalog's row is locked before the object, not after: a conditional grant locks the catalog for update and then
  the object, and an undrop locks the object and then its namespace, so any other order could deadlock with them.
- DR freezes are covered with holds: the claim and the removal read both the same way.
