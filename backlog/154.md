What to build: five-minute bars and live quotes (`brokerage.markets`) and sessions and click events
(`brokerage.digital`) on Kafka through the Iceberg sink in live mode; maintenance policies per namespace (short
snapshot retention and frequent compaction on the streaming tables, D30); the scale setting (accounts, days), with a

What done looks like: after a live day the health dashboard (D38) ranks the streaming tables among the neediest, and a

## What was done

The world now streams quotes, sessions and click events, demo.sh gives the streams' namespaces maintenance policies
of their own and keeps the held `aml_cases` out of every job that writes it, and the world's scale is a setting.

- The streams (`world/Quotes.java`, `world/Digital.java`, three new feeds): `brokerage.markets.quotes`, each
  security's best bid and offer through the session (09:30 to 16:00), the popular names every minute and the tail
  about one minute in five, a half-spread of 2 to 8 basis points around the price at that moment: 37,400 a business
  day at 300 securities. `brokerage.digital.sessions` and `.click_events`: about 0.35 sessions a business day per
  account (366 a day at 1,000 accounts, in the design's 200 to 500), weighted to active traders and short sellers,
  one in ten a visitor who never logs in, on web, iOS or Android, started 07:00 to 16:30, ended before 17:00; about
  14 events each (login, page and quote views, searches, order tickets, clicks, a logout half the time). Each event
  goes out at its time; a session's row as it ends. Each draws from a source of chance of its own, so every other
  feed is as before. `bars_5m` was world 1's already.
- Routes (D50): the three are ordinary Kafka feeds, so with Kafka off they go into SingleStore tables `quotes`,
  `sessions` and `click_events` (`demo/singlestore/brokerage-feeds.sql`, regenerated) and the one change feed
  `brokerage_world_to_lake` copies them into `brokerage.singlestore`. The world's sink takes their topics
  (`demo/brokerage/connector.json`).
- Maintenance policies (D30): `demo.sh`'s `MAINTENANCE_POLICIES`, set by `brokerage_maintenance` on every `gen` after
  the namespace grants. `brokerage.markets`: snapshots kept an hour, the newest alone beyond it; `brokerage.digital`:
  7 days; both compacted at 2 small files a partition and their manifests rewritten at 10, against `lakehouse`'s 5 and
  100 (`maintenance_policy`). `brokerage.compliance.aml_cases`: compaction and manifest rewrites off, set first.
- Governance: `brokerage_streams_governance` on every `gen`: `markets.quotes` internal, `brokerage.digital` internal,
  owned by `digital-channels`.
- The scale: `NUNA_DEMO_ACCOUNTS` (200 to 20,000), `NUNA_DEMO_SECURITIES` (50 to 1,000) and `NUNA_DEMO_CATCH_UP_DAYS`
  (1 to 30), defaults 1,000, 300 and 3, filled into `brokerage.yaml` and `brokerage-bootstrap.yaml`; `Main.settings`
  checks the bounds; the bootstrap fixes them once and warns when asked for another scale later. The generator's and
  its Jobs' memory follow the accounts (`world_memory`: 384 MB limit to 2,000 accounts, 64 MB more for each 1,000
  above; measured 37 MB of heap after six days at 1,000 accounts, 173 MB at 10,000). `docs/install-at-work.md`,
  "Sizing the brokerage world".
- Tests (fast build, no containers): `StreamsTest` (rates, order, shapes), `MaintenancePoliciesTest` (demo.sh's
  list against the test's, the catalog's own keys, the held table), `ScaleTest` (bounds, world size, demo.sh fills
  every value the generator's manifests name); `LakeSchemasTest`, `SingleStoreTablesTest` and `ConnectorsTest`
  cover the new feeds' columns, SingleStore tables and topics. `./gradlew :demo-datagen:test` passes.
- Bites: 9 in `bites/WORLD-154/`, each BITES; `bites/WORLD-152/owners-topic-in-the-worlds-sink.patch` recorded again
  against the new sink config, same break, BITES.

Choices made here, Paul may overrule:

- The click events' table is `click_events`, not the design's `events`: EVENT and EVENTS are keywords in MySQL's
  dialect, which SingleStore speaks and which creates the table unquoted while Kafka is off; not checked against
  SingleStore's own list.
- Quotes are a stream of their own, not the NBBO at each order event; digital sessions are not tied to the world's
  orders (an `order_ticket_open` names a security, never an order).
- `markets` keeps snapshots an hour (the least the catalog allows) and only the newest beyond; `digital` keeps 7 days
  as the design says, though that is longer than `lakehouse`'s hour.
- "Frequent compaction" is a lower threshold, not a more frequent run: compaction still runs once a night at most
  (D30 as amended 2026-11-15), and a threshold of 2 small files ranks the streams first.
- The held `aml_cases` gets a policy of its own turning compaction and manifest rewrites off, set as `presenter`, the
  only demo person with modify in `brokerage.compliance`; the hold's guardrail stops only expiry and orphan removal.
- The streams' SingleStore copies keep `lakehouse`'s policy: they exist only once their change feed has run.
- The scale's bounds (200 to 20,000 accounts, 50 to 1,000 securities, 1 to 30 days) and the memory formula; above
  10,000 accounts the memory is not measured.

Only the live demo can check (none of it was run against the stack):

- `demo/demo.sh gen`: the log shows `created namespace brokerage.digital`, `created table brokerage.markets.quotes`,
  `.digital.sessions`, `.digital.click_events`, and "the brokerage world's maintenance policies". Then, in Trino:
  `SELECT count(*) FROM lakehouse."brokerage.markets".quotes WHERE business_date = current_date` grows through the
  session to about 37,000; `SELECT count(*), count(brokerage_account) FROM lakehouse."brokerage.digital".sessions
  WHERE business_date = current_date` ends the day near 370, about 90% with an account.
- The policies: `GET /management/v1/catalogs/lakehouse/namespaces/brokerage%1Fdigital/maintenance-policy` resolves
  `snapshot-expiry.max-snapshot-age` P7D and `compaction.min-input-files` 2 from the namespace; on
  `.../namespaces/brokerage%1Fcompliance/tables/aml_cases/maintenance-policy`, `compaction.enabled` and
  `manifest-rewrite.enabled` false from the table, expiry and orphan removal false from the hold's guardrail.
- After a day of the generator: the console's Health page lists `brokerage.markets.quotes` and
  `brokerage.digital.click_events` among the neediest tables (small files, far past 2), and the night's compaction
  Jobs compact them; `aml_cases`' snapshot list (its table page, or `SELECT * FROM
  lakehouse."brokerage.compliance"."aml_cases$snapshots"` as `cora`) shows no `replace` snapshot and none expired.
- On an existing world (begun before this lands), the first `gen` sends the three streams from the world's first
  day as it catches up, then live.
- `NUNA_DEMO_ACCOUNTS=5000 demo/demo.sh gen` on a world already begun: the bootstrap's log says `WARN the world was
  fixed at 1000 accounts`; after `wipe` and `up`, `gen` begins a world of 5,000 (`brokerage` namespace's property
  `world.accounts`), and the generator's pod limit is 576Mi.
- With `kafka` off: `brokerage.singlestore.quotes`, `.sessions` and `.click_events` fill through the change feed.
- Uncertain: `digital`'s 7 days keep about 2,400 snapshots a business day in each of its two tables, so their
  metadata grows to several MB within the week, rewritten at each 15-second commit; watch the sink's and the
  catalog's commit times there.
