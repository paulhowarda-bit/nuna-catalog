On 2026-11-23 the demo's catalog was killed by the kernel at 18:59 UTC (`OOMKilled`, exit 137), 71 minutes after it
started, while the brokerage world (D48, item 150) had its Iceberg sink committing to five tables every 15 s. The
pod's limit is 768Mi and `JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS` is `-XX:MaxRAMPercentage=75`, so the heap may take 576Mi and everything
outside it (metaspace, code cache, thread stacks, direct buffers) has about 190Mi. The JVM never ran out of heap
(`ExitOnOutOfMemoryError` did not fire): the container went over its limit.

The sink's task died on the refused connection (`HttpHostConnectException ... nuna-catalog:8181`), and Kafka Connect
does not restart a failed task, so nothing reached the lake from then until it was restarted by hand at 20:39. The
generator gave up waiting for the sink and left 2026-12-02 to 2026-12-11 (simulated days) without their `eod-` tags.

Done looks like: the demo's catalog has 1Gi, with the heap at 60% of it (about 614Mi, a little more than before) and
about 410Mi outside it, set in `demo/k8s/catalog-values.yaml`, which `demo.sh` and `EngineMatrixTest` share. The
chart's own default (`deploy/helm/nuna-catalog/values.yaml`) stays as it is: production sizes are set at the install.

Done: `demo/k8s/catalog-values.yaml` gives the catalog a 1Gi limit, keeps its request at 768Mi, and sets
`javaToolOptions` to `-XX:MaxRAMPercentage=60 -XX:+ExitOnOutOfMemoryError`; `helm template` with the demo's values
renders them. A 1Gi request was landed first (71f5b284) and taken back: the demo's node has 94% of its memory
requested (7,296Mi of about 7,690Mi), and the new pod stayed Pending ("Insufficient memory") beside the one it
replaced. The sink task was restarted by hand at 20:39 UTC and committed to all five tables within a minute, until
the catalog was killed again at 20:42 under the catch-up load. At Paul's word the running catalog was then patched
by hand (`kubectl set resources`, `set env`, and the Recreate strategy so the new pod need not fit beside the old) and
the sink restarted at 21:00 UTC; the next `demo.sh up` puts the chart's values back.
