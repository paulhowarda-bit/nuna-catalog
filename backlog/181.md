Paul, 2026-11-23: "I want the data generator to continuously produce data like in real life." Asked how it should
behave outside the world's hours (every event falls between 07:00 and 17:00 New York time on a trading day), he chose
"Real clock, quiet off-hours" over adding after-hours events or spreading each day over 24 hours.

What was wrong: the generator played each business day's 08:00 to 17:00 in 10 minutes of wall time
(`BROKERAGE_DAY_MINUTES`), skipping nights and weekends, about 54 times faster than life. Stood up on 2026-11-23, by
20:40 UTC it had reached simulated 2026-12-16: data dated three weeks ahead of the calendar. And the sink's task died
for good each time the catalog restarted (backlog 179), so a world meant to run for days stopped reaching the lake
until someone restarted it by hand.

Done looks like:

- Each event is sent at its own business time on the real clock; an event whose time has passed (the catch-up days,
  today's earlier hours after a start or restart) goes at once. Nothing is sent overnight, at weekends or on holidays;
  a wait longer than five minutes is logged (`quiet until <time>`). `BROKERAGE_DAY_MINUTES` goes.
- The SingleStore writer survives a connection the server dropped while the world was quiet: a failed write
  reconnects and is tried once more.
- The generator checks the sink every minute through Kafka Connect's REST API (`CONNECT_URL`) and restarts its task
  when it has failed.
- `PacerTest` and `SinkWatchTest` cover the pacing and the failure check.

Done: `Pacer` sends each event at its own instant on the real clock (an injected `Clock` and sleeper in tests), logging
any wait over five minutes; `Main` drops `BROKERAGE_DAY_MINUTES` and starts `SinkWatch`, a daemon thread that reads
`/connectors/brokerage-lake/status` every minute at `CONNECT_URL` (`http://connect:8083` in `brokerage.yaml`) and asks
for `restart?includeTasks=true&onlyFailed=true` when a state is `FAILED`. `SingleStoreOrders` reconnects and writes
once more when a write fails. `PacerTest` (2) and `SinkWatchTest` (2) are new; `PublisherTest`'s encoding test built
its lot with `Map.of`, whose order changes between JVM runs, and failed on one; it now uses a `LinkedHashMap`. D48 is
amended, and `demo/README.md` and `docs/design/brokerage-world.md` describe the real clock. Checked: `demo-datagen`'s
tests and the fast build. Not yet seen live: the running demo's world was stood up on the compressed clock and has
sent its days to 2026-12-16, so on the real clock it would wait until that date; it needs a `demo.sh down` and `up`.
