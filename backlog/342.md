The heavy build of 2026-11-28 (by hand, 06:26 to 07:19) failed
`KubernetesCompactionTest#compactionRunsAsAJobPerTable` at line 144: the Job cut off by its 2 s deadline had
`status.failed` 1, but `{.status.conditions[?(@.type=='Failed')].reason}` was not `DeadlineExceeded`. The Ceph leg
ran the same test 7 minutes later and it passed (79 s).

Cause: the test, not the product or the kind cluster. Since Kubernetes 1.31 a Job that fails first sets
`FailureTarget` and counts its pod failed while the pod is still terminating, and sets `Failed` only once the pod is
gone, up to its grace period later. `CompactionCluster.await` returns as soon as `status.failed` is set, and the test
then read the `Failed` condition once. On a busy machine the pod takes longer to go and the read lands in the gap.
The product's `KubernetesJobs.state` already reads `FailureTarget` as well as `Failed`, so `CUT_OFF` is right either
way. The kind cluster runs v1.37.0. A Job with a 2 s deadline in the cluster, polled (`busybox sleep 60`):

```
07:30:00.181 ,1 term=1 conds=FailureTarget:DeadlineExceeded;
07:30:33.699 ,1 term=0 conds=FailureTarget:DeadlineExceeded;Failed:DeadlineExceeded;
```

Done when the test waits for the `Failed` condition (`CompactionCluster.awaitFailedReason`) and passes against the
kind cluster.

`CompactionCluster.awaitFailedReason` waits for the Job's `Failed` condition to carry a reason (Kubernetes 1.31 and
later set `FailureTarget` first, `Failed` up to a grace period later), and the test asserts on it.
`./gradlew :catalog-maintenance:test --tests KubernetesCompactionTest -Pheavy`: 1 test, 0 failures, BUILD SUCCESSFUL in
2m 30s (2026-11-28, 08:00).
