Done when every backend that partitions the journal keeps its horizon ahead without a migration, with a test.

Done: the four backends, checked in their schema steps and stores:
- PostgreSQL partitions the journal by month (V4). Before this, only `migrate()` extended it, twelve months ahead; a
  commit past the horizon lands in the default partition, so nothing fails, but the default grows, cannot be dropped
  by month, and each later extension moves its rows and scans it under an exclusive lock. Now the store keeps it:
  once a day at most, before a write that journals (commit, create, revive), it calls `journal_extend` through
  twelve months on from its clock. The day's first writer on each server does it; the others go on without waiting.
  It waits at most a second for each lock (`lock_timeout`, local to its transaction), since attaching a partition
  locks the default one exclusively and everything that reads it would queue behind. A failure is logged at WARNING
  and the write goes ahead; the next day's first write tries again. Two servers at once are safe: `journal_extend`
  serializes on the `journal_layout` row and creates only what is missing.
- Oracle partitions it by month with interval partitioning (V4): a commit past the horizon creates its month's
  partition itself, slowly (0.5 to 2.6 s on Free). V5's daily Scheduler job keeps this month and the next two
  ahead; `OracleStoreSuiteTest#schedulerJobCreatesJournalPartitionsAhead` (heavy) shows it. Nothing changed. Nothing
  notices the job when it cannot run: item 72.
- CockroachDB (in `catalog-store-sql`, suite in `catalog-store-sql-raft`) and MongoDB do not partition the journal:
  CockroachDB splits ranges by size (`JournalRangeSplits`, heavy), MongoDB keeps one collection. No horizon.

Why the write path and not a timer: it needs no thread, no caller and no change to the store interface or to
`catalog-server`, and it works in any process that writes the journal, whether or not it ever restarts. A store
that writes nothing needs no partitions. Plain PostgreSQL has no scheduler of its own without pg_cron; a Spring task
in the server would need a new `CatalogStore` method, no-ops in three stores, and a scheduled bean. The maintenance
worker reaches the catalog over REST only.

Tests: `JournalHorizonTest` (Java only, 8 tests: a clock the test moves, and PostgreSQL and CockroachDB faked at the
JDBC interface): the target is twelve months on in UTC; the first extension is due a day after the store is made
(the server migrates at start); one of sixteen concurrent writers extends; a commit, a create and a revive a day
later extend first (the commit with a one-second lock wait); a failed extension leaves the commit its own outcome and is tried
the next day; CockroachDB asks for nothing. `PostgresStoreSuiteTest#journalHorizonIsKeptWithoutAMigration` (heavy,
`JournalHorizonKept`): two servers migrate once and commit together monthly for fourteen months; every commit lands
in its month's partition, none in the default one, each month's partition once; then an extension behind an open
reader of the default partition gives up within a second, its commit lands, and the next day's commit extends.
