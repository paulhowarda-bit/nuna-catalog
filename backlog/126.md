Today the worker reports only each job's outcome, and the catalog keeps only each table's last compaction. So
neither a night's totals nor a full night can be drawn (wireframes.html, overview notes 2, 4 and 10).

What done looks like:
- The worker reports, for each job: start, end, slot, outcome, files in and out, snapshots expired and bytes deleted.
- The catalog keeps those reports for at least 30 nights, by an add-only schema step on all four stores.
- `GET /management/v1/maintenance/nights?days=` answers each night's totals, and each night's jobs with their slots.
- Store suite and route tests. The worker's report is tested the way `NightlyCleanupTest` tests the window.

What was done (docs/observability/maintenance-nights.md has the whole of it):

- **The worker** reports each job with `PUT /management/v1/maintenance/jobs/{id}`, as the maintenance principal. Each
  report gives the night, the window, the kind, the object, the slot, the start, end and outcome, files in and out,
  snapshots expired, and files and bytes deleted.
  - Compaction Jobs report as they start and as they end, on the lowest free slot of 1 to `max-jobs`, under an id made
    from the Job's name. Files in and out come from the table's `replace` snapshots on main since its assessment.
  - Orphan scans and purges report once they have ended.
  - Expiry, manifest rewrite and deletions are added up per table, kind and night, with failures kept apart.
- **The catalog** keeps the reports in `maintenance_job`, added by one add-only step on each store: PostgreSQL V12,
  CockroachDB V11, Oracle V13 and MongoDB step 11.
- **The read** is `GET /management/v1/maintenance/nights?days=&jobs=`.
- **Tests:**
  - `NightSuite` on all four stores (heavy);
  - `MaintenanceJobTest`, `MaintenanceNightsTest` and `NightJobsTest` (fast);
  - `MaintenanceNightsRoutesTest` (heavy);
  - night assertions in `NightlyCleanupTest` (heavy).
- **Bites:** twelve, in `bites/D30/`.

The choices, settled on D30's recommendation:

- **A night** is one opening of the cleanup window and the day after it, named by the date it opened in the window's
  zone. Work done round the clock counts to the night that opened last before it started.
- **Round-the-clock jobs are added up.** Expiry, manifest rewrite and deletions run at every scan, so a row per run
  would be up to 144 rows a table a day. Each table, kind and night gets one job for its runs that succeeded and one
  for its runs that failed. The worker reports each sum under one id as it grows, so a report repeated late does no
  harm. A run that succeeded and did nothing is not reported. The sums are kept in memory: a worker restarted reports
  its later runs under new ids, and the totals are unchanged.
- **Who reads what.** Anyone signed in reads the totals, which are counts of the worker's own work. A listed job names
  its table only to a caller who may `select` it, as the neediest-tables list does (D38).
- **`days`** is the newest N nights that have a job, up to tomorrow by the catalog's clock in UTC. The default is 30,
  clamped to 1 to 32.
- **`jobs`** is how many of the newest nights also list their jobs. The default is 1: last night's, for the overview
  chart. Only compaction Jobs and orphan scans are listed, since those are what the chart draws. Round-the-clock work
  is in the totals.
- **Retention.** The catalog keeps today's date in UTC and the 31 before it, which is at least 30 whole nights in any
  zone. Each report deletes older nights; no scheduled job is needed.
- **Which report wins.** A report replaces the kept one unless the kept one ended later, or ended while this one has
  not. The earlier start is kept, so a Job found after a restart keeps its first start. The night, kind and object stay
  as first reported.
- **Compaction files in and out** are read from the table's `replace` snapshots on main once the Job ends. The count
  runs back to the snapshot assessed that night, and no further back than an hour before the Job started. The Spark
  Job reports nothing of its own, and reading the snapshots needs no change to the Job.
- **Bytes deleted.** A purge takes the sizes from the listing it already makes. A deletion lists each file under its
  own key, because the worker's identity may list and delete but never read (D21, D30). That costs one LIST a file.
- **The kept object** may be a table or view that is active, dropped or purged, so a purge can be reported. An unknown
  object is 404. There is no foreign key: a job outlives its table's purge.
- **The night's date** is a DATE in the SQL stores, and `YYYY-MM-DD` text in MongoDB, which sorts the same way.
