Paul compared the live console with Atelier's prototype on 2026-11-24 and found it doesn't look like it; one of two
causes is that the demo runs no maintenance ("fix both").

`demo/README.md` says the demo has no maintenance worker, and catalog `lakehouse`'s policy leaves snapshot expiry,
orphan removal, compaction and manifest rewrite `false` (the built-in defaults). Live Overview says "Maintenance is
off in lakehouse's policy: nothing will be compacted or expired"; the "Every table, one mark" chart is flat, and
Queued for tonight, Last night and the 30-night trends are empty. In the prototype they are full, and they carry
Atelier's design. The brokerage world's Kafka Connect sink commits every 15 s, so its tables gather small files.

The demo's node had 7,296 MiB of its 7,686 MiB requested on 2026-11-24 with `ss` and `gen` on (about 6.3 GB in
use): a worker at the chart's 2 GiB, or a compaction Job whose request equals its limit (`KubernetesJobs` sets both
to `compaction.memory`), does not schedule.

Done when `demo/demo.sh up` runs the maintenance worker (`catalog-maintenance`, chart `deploy/helm/nuna-maintenance`)
with compaction Jobs in a namespace of their own, `lakehouse`'s policy turns every job on, the worker fits the demo's
Docker budget, the brokerage world's tags, branches, holds and freezes stay as they are, `demo/README.md` says so, and
the live console's Overview and Maintenance show a scan, a queue and a night.

Done (2026-11-24, local_e4d5a556): `demo.sh up` turns every job on in `lakehouse`'s policy as `ana` (snapshots
expired at an hour old, the least the catalog allows, keeping 5; the rest as built in) and installs the worker,
release `nuna-maintenance` in `nuna-demo` (`demo/k8s/maintenance-values.yaml`), with its own Keycloak client and
service account (the catalog's maintenance principal, group `demo-maintenance` with `write` on `lakehouse`), its own
