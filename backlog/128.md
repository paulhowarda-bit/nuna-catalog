The prototype's governance summary shows, for each catalog (D45):
- how many tables are classified;
- the holds and the DR freezes;
- the tables closed until classified.

It lists every hold and freeze with who placed it, when, why and when it was lifted.

Today governance is read one object at a time, so a summary would take one call per table. Holds and freezes are
placed and released one target at a time (`POST` and `DELETE .../holds`, `PUT` and `DELETE .../dr-freeze`).

What done looks like:
- `GET /management/v1/catalogs/{catalog}/governance/summary`, with those counts.
- A list of the catalog's holds and freezes, current and lifted, each with its reason as recorded.
- Route tests, including who may read it.

**What was done** (branch `worktree-agent-a97c480c79f42ac53`):

  recorded, who placed it and when, and for a lifted one who lifted it and when.

**Choices settled** (none is Paul's to make; each follows the ledger):
1. **The lifted history comes from the audit topic**, read as `GET .../audit` reads it: the last 20,000 offsets of
   each partition, then the catalog's hold changes and `dr-freeze` changes, each release or removal paired with its
   placing or setting. A released hold and a lifted freeze leave the store (`releaseHold` deletes the row,
   `removeAttribute` the freeze), so only their audit records keep them (D23). Keeping them in the store would be a
   schema step on all four stores, which item 126 holds; the queryable audit tables are read by one named group (D23)
   and need item 125's reader. The answer says how far it read (`history.since`, `complete` and each partition's
   offsets), so it never claims more than it holds. In a busy deployment the reads fill the topic and that is minutes,
   not the prototype's 30 days: item 133 keeps the history in the store.
2. **Who may read both: describe on the catalog**, as `GET .../governance` asks describe on its target. Both name
   namespaces and tables across the whole catalog, and only a grant on the catalog covers every name (D18, names
   hidden). A grant admin reads them (manage-grants on every catalog implies describe); a platform operator, who
   holds no grant, is refused with 403 ("Catalogs, not objects"), keeping the freeze routes and the DR readout. A
   caller that holds nothing in the catalog gets the missing catalog's 404 (item 68). Every refusal is a `Denial`.
3. **Classified counts an inherited classification; closed follows the authorizer.** A table is classified when it or
   a level above it has one, as its labels carry it (D27, amended 2026-11-12). It is closed when a level on or above
   it is regulated and it has no classification of its own, which is what `Authorizer` refuses (D27); so a table can
   be both. `GovernanceSummaryTest` asks the authorizer about every table and checks the two agree.
4. **Tables only are counted**, as the prototype counts them; views are walked so their holds are listed.
5. **A regulated area** is a catalog, namespace or table whose own regulated flag is true.
6. **A lifted freeze's reason** is its latest setting before the removal, as the store keeps only the latest. A lift
   whose placing is older than the read has a null placer and time, and a freeze's also a null reason.
7. **The history read is not audited as an audit read** (`AuditRead`): it returns only hold and freeze changes, the
   governance the standing list shows to the same callers. `GET .../audit` stays the only trail read.
8. **No per-caller limit**, unlike `GET .../audit` (item 19): the console reads it when a person opens the Governance
   page; each read is bounded by the 20,000 offsets and 10 seconds.
9. **Without the audit topic, or when it cannot be read**, the route still answers 200 with the standing holds and
   freezes, and `history` says `"available": false` with a reason.
