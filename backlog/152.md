## What was done

The world (demo-datagen) now has a back office beside the ledger: the parties behind its accounts and their KYC
records, a payments system that sees every money movement the ledger books, ACATS transfers, and compliance. The CDC
leg's PostgreSQL and Oracle hold its books of record in place of the toy `settlement.instructions` and `PAYMENTS`.
Recorded in `status/2026-11-26-backlog-152.md`.

- **Back office** (`world/BackOffice.java`, drawing from a random source of its own, so every feed before it is
  unchanged): parties, account roles, CIP checks, suitability profiles, beneficial owners and trusted contacts, written
  at the world's first day, each dated as its account opened, then changes of address, contact, employment and
  suitability each business day. Payments, ACH returns and ACATS transfers carry the ledger's own ids, amounts and
  dates (templates 30 to 35, 70 to 77 and 82 to 85; tie 5's raw material). A synthetic sanctions list of twelve
  entries in OFAC's SDN.CSV columns (`DEMO-SDN-2026-11`), screened at onboarding and on every wire. AML monitoring:
  structuring (three or more cash deposits from $8,000 to under $10,000 in five business days) and wires of $100,000
  or more, with alerts, cases and SARs. Wash-trade surveillance of each trade date at 07:15 the next morning.
  Complaints. Every identifier is synthetic (tax ids in area 666, EINs with prefix 00, phones 555-01xx, routing numbers
  starting 50).
- **Seeded**: three structuring cash deposits on played days 1 to 3 (an alert, escalated, and a case closed
  `sar_filed`), and a wire from list entry 9101 on day 2: a confirmed match, blocked, never booked by the ledger, with
  a `sanctions_block` case closed `report_filed`.
- **Fifteen new feeds and topics** (`brokerage.party.*`, `brokerage.money-movement.*`, `brokerage.compliance.*`) into
  `brokerage.party` (six new tables), `brokerage.money_movement` and `brokerage.compliance`. Each versioned feed (a
  party, suitability profile, trusted contact, payment, AML alert or case, surveillance alert, complaint) sends a new
  row per change with its version, and has a `<table>_current` view (`live/Current.java`) reading both routes' tables.
- **SingleStore route**: the compliance feeds' copies go under `brokerage.compliance.singlestore`, through a per-table
  namespace in the change feed (`iceberg.namespace.<table>`, singlestore-change-feed), so they inherit compliance's
  grants.
- **CDC leg**: PostgreSQL `backoffice` (schemas `party`, `money_movement` and `compliance`, thirteen tables) and Oracle
  `TREASURY.BANK_PAYMENTS` (each ACH and wire as the firm's bank sees it). `Main cdc` (was `Main custody`) loads all
  three databases at each `demo.sh cdc` with what changed since the last load (`live/Sources.java`); Debezium writes
  `history.postgres.<schema>.<table>` and `history.oracle.treasury.bank_payments`. `cdc` waits for each snapshot (as
  many rows as each table holds) and makes no sample changes of its own any more.
- **Governance** (demo.sh `brokerage_backoffice_governance`, on every `gen`): party's new tables restricted,
  `money_movement` confidential and `compliance` restricted, both regulated and owned; a legal hold on
  `compliance.aml_cases`; `party.beneficial_owners` lands unclassified through a sink of its own,
  `brokerage-lake-owners`, which the generator restarts once a steward classifies the table. The history namespaces
  are classified as their lake namespaces. lakehouse's grants for `demo-analysts` and `demo-stewards` moved from the
  catalog to its namespaces (`lake_grants`), so only `demo-compliance` reads a namespace named `compliance` and all
  below it.
- **cora**, in group `demo-compliance`: Keycloak, the console's token (`Mint.java`), CloudBeaver, kafbat (the
  compliance topics, read only; nobody else reads them), PostgreSQL (schema `compliance` only), Oracle and
  CockroachDB. `presenter` is in `demo-compliance` too.
- **Tests**: `BackOfficeTest` (3), `SourcesTest` (4), `CurrentTest` (1) and `ConnectorsTest` (4) are new;
  `SinkWatchTest`, `SingleStoreTablesTest`, `IcebergLakeTest`, `PublisherTest` and `ChangeFeedTaskTest` are extended.
  `./gradlew :demo-datagen:test :singlestore-change-feed:test` passes: 125 and 39 tests, none failed.
- **Bites**: sixteen in `bites/WORLD-152/`, all BITES with the tree restored (`bites/run.sh bites/WORLD-152`,
  2026-11-27).

Choices made here, each one Paul may overrule:

- **The sources hold the end state; there is no backfill of the histories**: PostgreSQL and Oracle hold each record's
  latest version after the lake's last business day, loaded at each `cdc` with only what changed, as CockroachDB does
  (item 151). The history tables therefore start at the first `cdc` with one snapshot row per record, and each later
  `cdc` adds the changes since. Nothing writes the world's earlier versions into `history.*` in the sink's shape; the
  full version history is in the lake's own tables (`brokerage.party.parties` and so on). A choice made here, Paul may
  overrule.
- **No D20 flags** on tax ids, birth dates, addresses or bank account numbers: a flagged table opens its files only to
  trusted engines, and the Iceberg sinks and SingleStore's change feed that write these tables are not one, so
  flagging them would stop the writes. **A question for Paul**: flag them anyway (registering the writers as trusted
  engines, or accepting that writes stop), flag only some tables, or leave them unflagged. Nothing is built for it
  until he answers.
- **beneficial_owners has its own sink**: a sink stops every table's commits at one it may not write, so the
  unclassified table has `brokerage-lake-owners`, restarted when the table is classified at its own level (an
  inherited classification does not count, D27). Its SingleStore copy is classified with the rest, so only the Kafka
  route shows the refusal. A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
- **Grants per namespace** (`lake_grants`): with no DENY, `ana`'s and `stella`'s groups lose their catalog-wide
  grants in lakehouse and get one on each namespace that neither holds nor is a compliance namespace. A namespace
  `ana` makes at the top of lakehouse gets her grant only at the next `up`, `gen` or `cdc`. A choice made here, Paul
  may overrule.
- **The compliance copies go under `brokerage.compliance.singlestore`**, not `brokerage.singlestore`, so they inherit
  compliance's grants; this needed a per-table namespace in the change feed. A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
- **The history namespaces are classified, not regulated**: a regulated namespace would close each history table to
  the sink that makes it until a steward classified it. A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
- **cora's password** is made per machine like every person's (backlog 282), not `cora-demo` as this item says, and
  `presenter` joins `demo-compliance`. A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
- **Database grants**: in PostgreSQL only `cora` and the superusers read `compliance`, and `stella` and `oscar` read
  `party` and `money_movement` (no longer `pg_read_all_data`). Oracle and CockroachDB give `cora` what `stella` has.
  A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
- **A leg begun before this item** (no `world=152` in configmap `cdc-begun`) starts anew once, losing the toy tables.
  A choice made here, Paul may overrule.
