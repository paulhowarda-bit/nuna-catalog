Tour the console

Visit every page of the console once, on the local demo's catalog lakehouse, signed in as presenter: find a table, read its tabs, look at last night's maintenance, a legal hold, a grant, an audit record and the Service page. At the end you know where everything in the console lives. It takes about 20 minutes.

Demo only For everyone who will use the console

What you need

  • The local demo running. Run nuna on your laptop starts it.
  • The console open at https://localhost:8181/ui/, with the demo's certificate trusted.
  • The password of presenter, from ~/.nuna-demo/people. presenter belongs to every demo group, so every page and button appears.

Sign in and look around

  1. Select Sign in with the demo's Keycloak, and sign in as presenter.

    Keycloak's page signs you in and sends you back. Signing in to the console covers the other way in, the token box.

  2. Read the header.

    From the left: the menu button, nuna, the Catalog picker showing lakehouse, the Reading chip, Go to a path, who you are with your groups and when your sign-in ends, the theme switch and Sign out.

  3. Read the Overview.

    The console opens on Overview, since presenter reads data, the audit feed and operates the platform. The title says how many of the tables you may read are in shape. Every table, one mark draws one mark per table, and Queued for tonight lists what the worker will compact first.

The rail lists eight pages

The rail shows Overview, Catalog, Branches and tags, Maintenance, Governance, Grants, Audit and Service. A person in fewer groups sees fewer: The console says who sees which.

Find a table and read its tabs

  1. Press / and type xetra trades.

    Go to a path matches every word in any order across every catalog. Press Enter on markets.equities.eu.xetra.trades.

  2. Read the Overview tab.

    The head marks the table Held and classified internal. The figures give its rows, data files, snapshots and schema changes; Current state names its current snapshot and metadata file.

  3. Select Schema.

    The table's schemas are listed. Pick two to see the fields added between them. Its partition specs are below.

  4. Select History.

    Commits lists every commit, newest first, with who made it. Select a snapshot, then Copy time-travel SQL to copy a query that reads the table as it was then.

  5. Select Maintenance.

    Assessment gives the worker's last verdict and score, and Policy for this table shows each setting with where it was set.

The table is under a legal hold

On the Overview tab, the Governance panel names the legal hold placed by stella.

Look at a hold and a freeze

  1. On the same table, select Access.

    The governance panel says 1 legal hold: "Snapshots cannot be removed; it cannot be dropped or purged." Legal holds gives its reason, "Matter 2026-114: trade surveillance review", with Release. Do not release it.

  2. Select Governance in the rail.

    Holds and freezes lists the hold and a DR freeze on the namespace reference, placed by oscar. Regulated areas and Flagged tables follow; markets.desks.positions is flagged with two rules.

One hold and one freeze stand

The Summary tab's Legal holds and DR freezes figures each count one.

Read through a branch

  1. Select Branches and tags in the rail.

    The demo has the branch dev, which changed two tables, and the tag sample.

  2. Select Merge… on dev.

    The Merge tab runs a dry run and says whether dev could merge. Do not select Merge @dev into main: Work on a catalog branch does that.

  3. Open the Reading chip and choose dev.

    A band says you are reading the branch. Open markets.fx.rates: its rows include the rates committed on dev. Select Read main instead to go back.

The header says main again

The Reading chip no longer names dev.

Look at last night's maintenance

  1. Select Maintenance in the rail.

    The four figures cover the last 30 nights. Tonight's queue lists the tables queued, equality deletes first. Last night draws each job in its slot.

  2. Read Policy for lakehouse.

    Each key shows its value and who set it. Edit the catalog's policy changes it; leave it as it is.

No setup checklist appears

The demo's worker has everything it needs, so What the worker needs to maintain lakehouse is not shown. A new install shows it until all four steps are done.

Make a grant

  1. Select Grants in the rail.

    Groups lists each group that holds a grant in lakehouse, with its duties.

  2. Select Grant access.

  3. Type the group demo-readers.

    The catalog cannot list your directory's groups, so it takes a new name as typed.

  4. Choose Read its data, find the namespace markets, and read the line beneath.

    It says what demo-readers will be able to do.

  5. Select Grant.

The grant is made and recorded

The dialog says Granted select on markets to demo-readers. and "It is in the audit trail." To undo it, list the grants of demo-readers and select Revoke.

Read an audit record

The demo configures no audit archive

So the hash chain line reads "no archive is configured", and Hash chain says "No archive is configured on this server, so no chain is checked." On an install with an archive, Check this record shows the record's place in its server run's chain.

  1. Select Audit in the rail.

    Records reach the audit tables about a minute after they happen.

  2. Choose the grants kind, and open your grant's record.

    The record shows the full event, its request id, where it was read from and its hash chain line.

Your grant is in the feed

The record names presenter, demo-readers and select. If the status line says nothing has been committed to the audit tables, wait a minute and select the kind again.

Look at the Service page

  1. Select Service in the rail.

    The title says how many servers answered and whether every latency objective was met over the last day. Below are audit copying, credential vending, alerts, servers, the worker's scans and storage, DR replication and the store.

The catalog picker is gone

The Service page covers the whole deployment, so the header shows no catalog.

What you did

You signed in through the demo's identity provider and visited every page in the rail. You found a table with Go to a path and read its Overview, Schema, History, Maintenance and Access tabs, including a legal hold. You read the catalog through a branch, looked at the maintenance queue and policy, granted select to a new group and found that grant's audit record. Next, work on a catalog branch.
