Concepts

The ideas behind nuna, in reading order: what the parts are, how a commit is decided, and how access, storage, audit and maintenance work.

Each page explains one mechanism: what it is, how it works, what it guarantees and what it does not, and where you see it. Read the first four in order if you are new to nuna; the rest stand on their own.

  • How nuna fits together The server, its database, the object store, the maintenance worker, the audit pipeline, the console and engines, and who talks to whom.
  • Catalogs, namespaces, tables and views The object model, how names are compared, how a table's location is assigned, and the REST prefix.
  • How a commit works Requirements and updates, how the database decides one winner, retries, idempotency and multi-table transactions.
  • Catalog branches and tags Refs of a whole catalog: what a branch keeps, how a merge is judged, and when a branch expires.
  • Storage profiles and credential vending How the server maps a table to an object store and prefix, and the short-lived credentials an engine receives.
  • Identity and access Principals, groups, the seven privileges, grants and how they pass down the tree, and separation of duties.
  • Row filters and column masks Per-group rules on flagged tables, who may see them, and why a flagged table's storage access is withheld.
  • Governance Regulated and flagged tables, legal holds and DR freezes: what each blocks and who may place or lift it.
  • Dropping, restoring and purging Why every drop is soft, how long you have to restore, and what a purge deletes.
  • The audit trail What is recorded and when, the hash-chained archive, and the audit tables you can query with SQL.
  • Maintenance Scans, the health score, compaction on need, snapshot expiry, orphan scans and the cleanup window.
  • Disaster recovery The DR watermark, the verifier, and how tables are brought back to a version the DR region holds whole.
  • Choosing a database PostgreSQL, CockroachDB, Oracle and MongoDB: what each needs and what the server checks at start-up.
