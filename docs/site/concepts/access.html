Identity and access

Identity and access is how nuna knows who a request comes from, by a token from your identity provider, and what that principal may do, by grants of privileges to its groups.

What it is

A principal is who a request acts as: a person signed in to the console, a person using an engine, or a program such as the maintenance worker. The server learns the principal from a bearer token issued by an identity provider you trust. The token also carries the principal's groups, and the groups decide what it may do.

What a group may do is a set of grants. A grant gives one privilege on one object to one group. There are seven privileges: describe, select, write, modify, create, manage-grants and manage-tags.

How it works

Issuers and groups

Each trusted identity provider is an issuer in nuna.security.issuers, with the claims that carry the user id, the groups and the client id. The server keeps no list of people and no one's password, and it never reads the directory: if the identity provider puts a group in the token, the principal is in that group for that request. See Authentication.

Grants and inheritance

A request needs one privilege on the object it touches. The principal holds it when one of its groups holds that privilege, or one that includes it, on the object or anything above it. modify includes write, write includes select, and every privilege includes describe. Grants only add: there is no DENY.

Separation of duties

In one catalog, one group may not hold duties that conflict. A group that manages grants holds nothing else, and a group that changes data does not also set tags. The server refuses a grant that would break this, and no one may grant to a group they belong to. The rules and their refusals are in Authorization.

Roles from the settings

Three roles come from the server's settings, not from grants:

Grant admins
Members of a group in nuna.security.grant-admin-groups. They may grant and revoke in every catalog and hold no other grant.
Platform operators
Members of a group in nuna.security.platform-operator-groups. They run the deployment (trusted engines, DR freezes, the worker's storage) and hold no grant at all.
Maintenance principals
Principals listed in nuna.security.maintenance-principals, acting as themselves. Only they may call the worker's endpoints.

No role reads or changes data by itself. There is no superuser.

What it guarantees and what it does not

  • Every request is decided for its principal, or for the end user a trusted engine names, and every refusal leaves an audit record.
  • A principal that holds nothing on an object cannot tell whether it exists: the server answers as for a missing name.
  • Who is in a group is the identity provider's business. Removing someone from a group takes effect when their current token expires.

What a refusal tells you

A refusal answers 403 and names what was missing, for example subject holds no privilege that gives select for loadTable, or the rule that refused. A missing name answers 404, whether the object is missing or hidden from you. Error responses lists every status.

Where you see it

In the console, the Grants page shows a catalog's grants to those who manage them, and every object's Access tab shows What you may do. The endpoints are in the Grants API.

Limits

  • Grants go to groups only; a grant cannot name a person.
  • A grant that names a catalog branch can only give write or modify.
