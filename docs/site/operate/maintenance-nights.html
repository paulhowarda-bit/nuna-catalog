Maintenance nights

When the maintenance worker does each kind of work, how to set its cleanup window, and how to read what each night did.

For platform operators

The maintenance worker scans the catalog every scan-interval, ten minutes by default. Some work runs at every scan; the heavy work waits for the cleanup window, a stretch of each night you choose. A night is one opening of the window and the day that follows, until the window next opens. It is named by the date the window opened on, in the window's zone.

Before you begin

  • The maintenance worker installed (Install the maintenance worker).
  • Your deployment's quiet hours, and the time zone they are in.

What runs when

The worker's work, by when it runs
WorkWhen
Snapshot expiryAt every scan, round the clock.
Manifest rewriteAt every scan, round the clock.
Purges of dropped objects whose window has endedAt every scan, round the clock.
Deleting files whose deletion is dueAt every scan, round the clock.
Compaction assessment and compaction JobsOnly in the cleanup window. Each table gets at most one Job a night, the neediest first, at most compaction.max-jobs at once. Each Job stops at the window's end.
Orphan scansOnly in the cleanup window, at most once per table per orphan-interval (one night by default).

Round-the-clock work counts towards the night that opened last before it started.

Set the cleanup window

The window has no default: the worker needs start, end and zone. Set them in the maintenance chart's values:

config:
  nuna:
    maintenance:
      cleanup-window:
        start: "22:30"
        end: "04:00"
        zone: Europe/London
  • start and end are local times in zone, a named time zone.
  • An end before the start crosses midnight.
  • An end equal to the start keeps the window open all day.

Quote the times

YAML 1.1 reads an unquoted 22:30 as the number 1350. Write "22:30".

Other settings that shape the night are on Maintenance worker settings: nuna.maintenance.scan-interval (PT10M), nuna.maintenance.orphan-interval (P1D) and nuna.maintenance.compaction.max-jobs (2). Which tables need compaction is maintenance policy, set in the catalog (Table health).

Read last night in the console

The overview's Maintenance, last night figure shows the newest night's tables compacted, small files merged, snapshots expired, unreferenced files deleted and jobs cut off at the window's end, for every catalog. Select it to open the Maintenance page.

The Maintenance page shows four figures with a sparkline over the nights reported, and a Last night panel that draws each compaction Job on its slot and the orphan scans in a lane of their own, with a dashed line at the window's end. Under it are the counts Compaction jobs finished, Cut off at the window's end, Failed and Still running.

Anyone signed in sees the totals. A job names its table only to someone who may select it; others see the job without a name.

Read the nights with the API

Open the Maintenance page from the rail. The page reads the last 30 nights and the newest night's jobs.

Call GET /management/v1/maintenance/nights (curl, reference):

curl -sS "$NUNA/management/v1/maintenance/nights?days=7&jobs=1" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN"
  • days: how many nights to answer, the newest first. Only nights with at least one job count. Default 30, held to 1 to 32.
  • jobs: how many of those nights carry their jobs, the newest first. Default 1, held to 0 to days.

A night's totals

FieldMeaning
nightThe date the window opened on, in the window's zone.
opens, closesThe window as the worker saw it.
tables-compactedCompaction Jobs that succeeded.
files-compacted, files-writtenData files the night's compaction Jobs replaced, and those they wrote.
snapshots-expiredSnapshots expired over the whole night.
files-deleted, bytes-deletedFiles deleted, and their bytes, by purges and due deletions. A file counts when it is deleted, not when it is listed for deletion.
jobs-running, jobs-succeeded, jobs-cut-off, jobs-failedJobs of every kind, by how they stand. Round-the-clock work counts once for each table and kind.

A night's jobs

Only compaction Jobs and orphan scans are listed, by start. Round-the-clock work counts in the totals only.

FieldMeaning
idThe job's id. A compaction's id comes from its Kubernetes Job's name, so a restarted worker reports the same Job under the same id.
kindcompaction or orphan-scan.
slotA compaction's slot, from 1 to compaction.max-jobs. Absent for an orphan scan.
started-at, ended-atWhen the worker started the job, and when it saw it end. No ended-at while it runs.
outcomerunning, succeeded, failed, or cut-off when the window's end stopped it.
files-in, files-outFor a compaction, the data files it took out and those it wrote. 0 for an orphan scan.
tableThe table, only to a caller who may select it. Absent once the table is purged.

In the demo

The demo's window opens at "12:00" and ends at "12:00", America/New_York, so it is open all day and every scan may compact. Its catalog is lakehouse.

If it fails

  • The worker does not start, or never compacts. Check that cleanup-window names start, end and zone, with quoted times. Compaction also needs compaction.image and a namespace for the Jobs (Install the maintenance worker).
  • Jobs are cut off night after night. The window is too short for the work, or compaction.max-jobs too low. NunaCompactionFallingBehind fires after 3 days of it.
  • The console says the worker has reported no job in the last 30 nights. The worker is not running or cannot reach the catalog. See NunaWorkerNotScanning.
  • The route answers 500. A jobs value that is not a number is answered 500 rather than 400. Send a whole number.

The catalog keeps the jobs in its database for 31 nights, so the figures survive restarts of the catalog servers. The worker adds up round-the-clock runs in memory for the night under way: a restarted worker reports its later runs under new ids, and the night keeps both.
