Dashboards and alerts

Install the three Grafana dashboards and the 29 Prometheus alert rules that ship with nuna, and read what each watches.

For platform operators

The files are in deploy/grafana/. They read nuna's metrics as Prometheus names them when it receives them over OTLP with its default translation (Metrics).

The files in deploy/grafana
FileHolds
dashboards/nuna-catalog.json36 panels: the latency and availability objectives, loads, commits, credential vending, the audit's Kafka acknowledgement and archive, and nuna's alert counters.
dashboards/nuna-maintenance.json16 panels: the worker's scans, failures, work done and compaction.
dashboards/nuna-client-reports.json13 panels: engines' scan and commit reports, by engine.
rules/nuna-catalog.rules.yaml19 rules: two burn-rate rules for each of three latency and three availability objectives, three for the alert counters, four for absent metrics.
rules/nuna-maintenance.rules.yaml10 rules for the maintenance worker and its DR verifier.
tests/nuna-catalog.test.yamlpromtool unit tests of the catalog rules. It is not a rule file: keep it out of the folder Prometheus loads.

Before you begin

  • Both programs send metrics to a collector (Send telemetry), and Prometheus receives them over OTLP with names translated to underscores and suffixes, such as Prometheus' UnderscoreEscapingWithSuffixes strategy.
  • The charts set service.namespace, so each deployment's job label is namespace/nuna-catalog or namespace/nuna-maintenance.
  • Grafana with a Prometheus data source. The dashboards use core panels only and schema version 39.

Install the dashboards

  1. Import each file.

    In Grafana, open Dashboards, then New, then Import, and upload each JSON file.

  2. Pick the data source.

    Select your Prometheus in the datasource variable at the top. The files name no data source.

  3. Pick the deployment.

    Select the deployment in job. The catalog and client-reports dashboards also take catalog, and the reports dashboard engine.

Load the alert rules

Each rule file holds a groups: list. List the two files under rule_files: in Prometheus, or, with the Prometheus Operator, paste a file's groups: list unchanged under a PrometheusRule's spec.groups:

apiVersion: monitoring.coreos.com/v1
kind: PrometheusRule
metadata:
  name: nuna-catalog
spec:
  groups:
    - name: nuna-catalog-slos
      rules: []   # the file's rules, unchanged

Every rule has a severity label, page or warning, for Alertmanager to route on. Each alert's runbook is on Alert runbooks.

Name the deployments you expect

NunaCatalogGone names no deployment, and NunaCatalogSilent forgets a deployment after a day of silence. To always hear about a given deployment, add a rule of your own for its job:

- alert: NunaCatalogDeploymentGone
  expr: absent_over_time(nuna_catalog_alerts_metadata_integrity_total{job="prod/nuna-catalog"}[10m])
  keep_firing_for: 10m
  labels:
    severity: page

The objectives

The latency objectives read nuna.catalog.requests, whose only buckets are the bounds: 10, 50 and 300 ms. Each is read as a share within its bound, not as an interpolated quantile.

Service level objectives the rules watch

Not-modified latency: p99 under 10 msloadTable answered 3xx1% slower
Commit latency: p99 under 300 msupdateTable and commitTransaction answered 2xx1% slower
Load, commit and all-request availability5xx answers against 2xx, 3xx and 5xx; 4xx counts on neither side, 409 conflicts included0.1% answered 5xx

Fast burn rules page when the budget burns at 14.4 times its rate over 1 hour and 5 minutes, or 6 times over 6 hours and 30 minutes. They have no for and keep firing 10 minutes after their condition clears, so they overlap NunaCatalogGone when a server stops sending. Slow burn rules warn when the budget burns at its rate or faster over 3 days and 6 hours, for 15 minutes. Each objective is per deployment, summed over its servers and catalogs.

Few calls make big shares

With 10 commits an hour at night, one commit answered 5xx is 10% of the hour, past the 1.44% the fast rule fires at. One failure can page.

The dashboard also draws commits per second against a line at 500. No rule watches that rate: it depends on demand.

The maintenance rules compare with 600, the default scan interval in seconds, and two wait 20m, two intervals. If you change nuna.maintenance.scan-interval, change them to match.

If your Prometheus names differ

  • Timers in seconds. If Prometheus scrapes rather than receives OTLP, timers end _seconds_: change _milliseconds_, the bounds to le 0.01, 0.05 and 0.3, and 600 * 1000 to 600 in the worker's scan rule.
  • Names keep their dots. Under a translation without underscore escaping, every name and dotted label changes.
  • Native histograms. There are no _bucket series; the objective queries need histogram_fraction. NunaRequestSeriesMissing fires when this happens.
  • A collector scraped through a ServiceMonitor. Set honorLabels: true, or the series' job and instance arrive as exported_job and exported_instance.

In the demo

The demo runs Prometheus with its OTLP receiver and translation_strategy: UnderscoreEscapingWithSuffixes, fed by an OpenTelemetry collector, and Grafana with the three dashboards and both rule files loaded. Its job labels are nuna-demo/nuna-catalog and nuna-demo/nuna-maintenance. The audit acknowledgement row stays empty there because the demo audits to its log, not to Kafka.

If it fails

  • Panels are empty. Rates are over 5 minutes and metrics arrive each minute, so wait 5 minutes. Panels of failures stay empty until something fails.
  • Every objective rule is silent. Check NunaRequestSeriesMissing and NunaSloBucketMissing: the series may arrive under other names.
